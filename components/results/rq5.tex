\subsection{RQ5: Practitioner Perceptions of Goal Violations}
\label{ss:sec:rq5}

\reva{
    11 of 13 respondents (84.6\%) found the goal violation examples useful to their workflow, scoring their utility with a median of five on a five-point scale.
    Five respondents highlighted its utility for guiding further review, with one response putting it as ``a triage and discovery layer, but not \ldots a final judgment.''
    Three respondents highlighted the concrete goal-violating transaction sequences as useful for understanding the issue.
}

\reva{
    Despite the examples being deliberate features of the USDT contract, all respondents rated the issues with end-user-facing behaviors that goal violations uncover as relevant to security, with a median of five on a five-point scale.
    One respondent highlighted the context-dependent nature of whether a goal violation was concerning, noting that ``these violations on this particular contract don't mean much, but I can see these kinds of flags being useful when buying novel assets''.
    All respondents believed that most end-users were unaware of administrative risks such as pausing transfers or blacklisting addresses, with one respondent commenting that ``most users assume ‘decentralized’ and miss centralized admin controls entirely''.
}
